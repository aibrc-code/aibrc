# Protocol statement

## Born from Bitcoin's spirit and the promise of the Agent era

AIBRC grew from a love of Bitcoin, its ecosystem and the possibility of a new economy of people and Agents.
The protocol has no publicly designated team or central founder. It pays tribute to Satoshi Nakamoto's
peer-to-peer electronic cash and the builder's instinct behind it: **rules above personalities, mathematics
above promises**.

AIBRC is an open, nonprofit Web3 protocol experiment built around Bitcoin L1. Its specifications, evolution
and future direction are open to a decentralized community of developers, researchers, creators and Agents.
Independent implementations and public proposals give that openness a practical form.

## Don't trust, verify

Bitcoin's community expresses the principle in a short line: **Don't trust, verify.**
For AIBRC, that means open rules, inspectable bytes and results another implementation can reproduce.

> “cryptographic proof instead of trust”
>
> — Satoshi Nakamoto, [Bitcoin whitepaper, §1](https://bitcoin.org/bitcoin.pdf)

In his [2009 introduction to Bitcoin](https://satoshi.nakamotoinstitute.org/posts/p2pfoundation/1/), Satoshi
also wrote: **“Users hold the crypto keys to their own money”**. That spirit guides the protocol's attention
to explicit ownership, understandable authority and bounded delegation. The owner key and an Agent's working
mandate have different roles, and both should be visible in the rules.

Open verification belongs close to the person using the system. A wallet can explain an instruction, an
indexer can reproduce its state and a personal Agent can run the tools that check the result. Rules become
shared infrastructure when their interpretation is open to everyone.

## Acknowledgments and community stewardship

AIBTC Labs provides early sponsorship for the documentation website and foundational GitHub repository.
We thank the wallet builders, protocol contributors, creators and communities whose work keeps the Bitcoin
ecosystem moving. Ordinals inscriptions, BRC-20, Runes and the wider field of Bitcoin experiments are part of that
shared history.

The long-term direction is community-supported stewardship, funded through voluntary donations and openly
organized ecosystem treasuries. Future infrastructure and incentive arrangements can develop through public
proposals and community participation.

## Open creation

The protocol specification and documentation are dedicated under [CC0-1.0](../LICENSE-DOCS), in the public-domain
spirit of unrestricted learning and reuse. Reference code is available under [MIT](../LICENSE-CODE), with its
notice requirements; [third-party material](../THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md) retains its applicable licenses.

Developers, quantitative researchers, wallets, terminal platforms, people and autonomous Agents can adopt,
fork, integrate and build on this work under those licenses. Participation begins with reading the rules,
contributing an implementation, proposing an improvement or creating something useful.

## Experimental participation

AIBRC explores Bitcoin's programmable possibilities. Participants should understand the protocol rules,
cryptographic assumptions and market risks before signing transactions. Confirmed activity follows the
canonical chain and its confirmation model; applications explain their own service responsibilities.
The [trust model](../TRUST_MODEL.md) is the technical reference for those assumptions.

Bitcoin brings money and verifiable history. AIBRC brings common asset rules. People and Agents bring the
applications, cooperation and imagination that make an ecosystem worth building.
